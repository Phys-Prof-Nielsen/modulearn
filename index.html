About ModuLearn Academy

ModuLearn Academy is my personal educational website, created to share learning resources, explore instructional ideas, and organize educational materials around a modular approach to learning. It brings together my professional work as a physics professor at Utah Valley University (UVU) and my personal interests in languages, mathematics, programming, and other subjects.

The physics learning materials are developed as part of my teaching and curriculum development responsibilities at UVU. These resources reflect my ongoing efforts to improve physics education through structured instruction, clearly defined learning outcomes, and competency-based assessment. The materials are used in my university courses and are continually refined through teaching experience, student feedback, and educational research.

The other learning areas, including languages and programming, are primarily personal projects developed out of curiosity, enjoyment, and a passion for lifelong learning. They provide opportunities to explore new subjects, experiment with educational approaches, and share resources with others who have similar interests.

ModuLearn Academy is not intended to operate as a commercial educational institution. Rather, it serves as a personal collection of educational resources, teaching materials, and learning projects.


The Learning Sphere Approach

A central organizing concept of ModuLearn Academy is the Learning Sphere. Each sphere represents a subject area organized into progressive modules that provide a structured pathway through the material.

In physics, this structure reflects my tiered, competency-based approach to instruction and assessment, grounded in standards-based grading and Bloom's Taxonomy. Learning objectives guide students from foundational knowledge and conceptual understanding toward increasingly sophisticated applications, analysis, and synthesis.

Physics instruction and assessment are organized into four progressive phases:

  • Phase D – Foundations: Developing essential conceptual knowledge, mathematical tools, and fundamental problem-solving skills.

  • Phase C – Application: Applying physical principles to increasingly complex situations, including multidimensional problems and the integration of multiple concepts.

  • Phase B – Analysis and Reasoning: Developing deeper mathematical and conceptual reasoning, evaluating assumptions, and explaining the physical principles underlying solutions.

  • Phase A – Synthesis and Exploration: Integrating knowledge and skills to investigate challenging problems, develop physical models, and explore real-world applications.

This progression is reflected in both the instructional materials and the associated assessments. Students are encouraged to develop competency through practice, feedback, reflection, and opportunities for reassessment. The emphasis is on demonstrated understanding rather than simply accumulating points.

Although the other Learning Spheres may also use modular organization, they are not necessarily based on the same formal instructional and assessment structure as my university physics courses.


What You Will Find Here

  • Physics Education and Curriculum Development: Original instructional materials, learning outcomes, worked examples, practice problems, and competency assessments developed for my physics courses at Utah Valley University. These resources reflect my professional teaching philosophy and ongoing curriculum development.

  • Structured Learning Resources: Subject-specific Learning Spheres organized into modules that support progressive learning, independent exploration, and the development of new skills.

  • Languages and Personal Learning Projects: Resources and activities related to language learning and other subjects that I enjoy exploring outside my professional responsibilities.

  • Programming and Computational Exploration: Personal programming projects, computational experiments, and opportunities to explore how technology can support learning and problem-solving. Computational tools developed specifically for my physics courses are also part of my professional teaching work.

  • Educational Innovation: Explorations of instructional strategies, interactive learning tools, and AI-supported learning resources. Within my physics courses, these developments are part of my ongoing efforts to strengthen student engagement, independent reasoning, and conceptual understanding.


Teaching, Learning, and Curiosity

I view education as an ongoing process of exploration, reflection, and growth. My professional work in physics education and my personal learning projects are connected by a common interest in understanding how people learn and how complex ideas can be made more accessible.

ModuLearn Academy provides a space to organize and share these efforts. Some resources represent formal curriculum development for my university teaching, while others are simply projects pursued for the enjoyment of learning something new.

Whether you are a student, educator, or curious learner, I hope you find something here that encourages you to explore, question, and continue learning.

Learning does not end in the classroom. Curiosity, exploration, and the enjoyment of discovering something new are lifelong pursuits.
