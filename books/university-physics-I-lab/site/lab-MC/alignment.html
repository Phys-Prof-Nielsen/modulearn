Instructor / Researcher Only — Alignment with Monte Carlo Manuscript#

Manuscript: Introducing Uncertainty Propagation with Monte Carlo Methods in Introductory Physics Labs

Backward Design: Intended Learning → Assessment Evidence#

Intended Learning Outcome

Inventory Evidence

Planned Instructional Connection

LO1. Interpret experimental measurements and distinguish random variability from systematic effects.

Q1–Q3; S1–S2

Experimental measurement, repeated timing trials, uncertainty of the mean.

LO2. Explain how uncertainty in input quantities influences a derived result.

Q4–Q6; S3–S4

First-order analytical propagation for \(q=f(x,y)\); partial derivatives and sensitivity.

LO3. Represent uncertainty using distributions and recognize nonlinear transformations.

Q7–Q9; S5

Uniform and normal input distributions; output histograms and probability intervals.

LO4. Describe computational propagation and distinguish simulated samples from new observations.

Q10–Q11; S6–S8

Random sampling, repeated evaluation of the physical model, interpretation of MC output.

LO5. Interpret agreement between a measured result and a theoretical prediction in light of uncertainty.

Q12

Comparison of predicted and measured quantities; limitations of the uncertainty model.

Research Implementation#

Before Instruction#

Administer this pre-inventory, the background questions, and the confidence survey.

Students should not see the instructor scoring cell.

Instructional Intervention#

Complete the analytical propagation and Monte Carlo instructional notebook, followed by the authentic laboratory application.

Immediate Post-Assessment#

Reassess the same conceptual outcomes with matched items.

Preserve comparable scoring, but vary numerical values or physical contexts where appropriate.

Add separate performance tasks involving MC implementation and interpretation.

Delayed Post-Assessment (Approximately Two Months)#

Revisit a subset of core concepts using a different physical context to explore retention and transfer.

Methodological Notes for the Manuscript#

  • Describe the instrument as researcher-developed, not as a previously validated inventory.

  • Obtain expert feedback on content alignment, clarity, and distractors before administration.

  • Pilot the questions, if feasible, to identify ambiguity and unexpected interpretations.

  • Keep conceptual understanding, mathematics/programming preparedness, and self-reported confidence as distinct constructs.

  • Retain anonymous matched identifiers if paired pre/post analysis is planned and permitted under the approved research protocol.

  • Distinguish conceptual learning from implementation proficiency; recognizing what a simulation does is not the same as independently writing code.

  • MC propagates an assigned input uncertainty model. It does not automatically correct biased measurements or an incomplete physical model.

Interpretation Caution#

Any pre/post gain should be described in relation to the instructional intervention and study design.

Without an appropriate comparison condition, do not attribute the entire change uniquely to the Monte Carlo method rather than to instruction, repeated exposure, or the associated experimental activity.
