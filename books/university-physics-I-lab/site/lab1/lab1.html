Lab 1 (Synthetic): Hooke’s Law & Writing a Professional Jupyter Report#

Overview#

You’ll practice writing a professional lab report in Jupyter using a synthetic dataset on Hooke’s Law. Your job is to analyze the data, make publication-quality figures, perform a linear fit, report uncertainties, and write a clear, concise report that a peer could understand without seeing your code.

You’ll submit an exported HTML of your Jupyter notebook.

Learning goals#

  • Set up a clean Jupyter notebook with a title page, section headings, and references.

  • Use Markdown (with LaTeX) and code cells appropriately; format figures and tables professionally.

  • Import a CSV, perform a linear fit, show residuals, and quantify uncertainty in the slope.

  • Interpret the slope as the spring constant \(k\) and compare to a manufacturer’s spec with tolerance.

  • Write a short abstract, motivate the model, and discuss limitations/outliers.

Physics background (short)#

Hooke’s Law states that the linear spring force is (in N)

\[ F_s = kx, \]

where \(x\) is the displacement (or extension) from spring equilibrium (m), and \(k\) is the spring constant (N/m). In our data set, an applied force due to weight of various masses will stretch the spring from equilibrium. The force due to gravity is

\[ F_g = mg \]

where \(m\) is the applied mass and \(g = 9.89665~\text{m/s}^2\) is the acceleration of gravity. Measurement noise and anb outlier have been applied to the dataset to emulate real experiments.

Files Provided#

  • hookes_law_synthetic_lab.csv - measurements for Spring A and Spring B with repeated trials. Columns

    • spring (A or B), trial, mass_g, force_N, extension_m, u_extenstion_m (1 mm standard uncertainty).

  • hookes_law_specs.csv - manufacturer’s listed \(k\) and tolerance for each spring.

What to Produce (Your Report)#

Structure your notebook as a professional report. Use Markdown for prose and equations, and keep code focused and readable.

  1. Title (Markdown only)

    • Title, course, your name(s), date, short abstract (100–150 words).

  2. Abstract (Markdown only)

    • Example abstract elements: purpose, method (linear fit of \(F\) vs \(x\)), key results (values of \(k\) with uncertainty), and one-sentence conclusion.

  3. Introduction (Markdown, light LaTeX allowed)

    • One short paragraph: What is Hooke’s Law and why linear regression is appropriate.

    • Specify the model: \(F = kx\) with the expectation that intercept \(\sim0\)

  4. Methodology (Markdown, light LaTeX, and short code)

    • Summarize how you

      • Imported the CSV(s).

      • Selected columns and subset by spring.

      • Fit a straight line \(F = kx + b\) and obtained uncertainties for \(k\) and \(b\).

      • Made figures: labeled axes with units, appropriate captions and readable font sizes.

    • Keep code chunks minimal and readable. Avoid long, un-commented cells.

  5. Results (Markdown, code/figures/tables)

    • For each spring:

      • Scatter plot \(F\) vs \(x\), overlay best-fit line.

      • Residual plot (residual \(= F_{measured} - F_{fit}\) to assess linearity and outliers.

      • Report \(k \pm u_k\) and intercept \(b \pm u_b\). Comment on whether \(b\) is consistent with zero.

      • Present a small table (Markdown) of the final extracted constants for A and B with uncertainties.

  6. Comparison to Manufacturer (Markdown)

    • Using hookes_law_specs.csv, compute percent difference between your \(k\) and the spec.

    • Decide if each result is consistent with the spec given the tolerance (e.g., spec ±5%). State your criterion explicitly.

  7. Discussion (Markdown)

    • Identify any outlier(s) and justify whether to keep or exclude them (and why).

    • Comment on major uncertainty sources (instrument resolution, alignment, mass labeling).

    • Reflect on whether the residuals show any systematic trend (nonlinearity, hysteresis).

    • State at least one limitation and one improvement for a future experiment.

  8. Conclusion (Markdown)

    • 3–5 sentences: What did you measure? What are your final \(k\) values with uncertainty? Are they consistent with manufacturer specs? Big-picture takeaway.

  9. References (Markdown)

    • Cite any sources you used (textbook, class notes). Use a simple, consistent style.

Professional writing & formatting checklist#

  • Use section headings (Markdown) and keep paragraphs short.

  • Figures: informative captions, labeled axes with units, readable fonts.

  • Numbers: report with appropriate significant figures and units.

  • Equations: use LaTeX in Markdown (don’t screenshot).

  • Code: concise and commented; avoid huge monolithic cells.

  • Narrative first: Make it readable without executing code cells (the exported HTML should stand alone).

What to submit#

  • One exported HTML from Jupyter that includes your text, equations, tables, figures, and code.
