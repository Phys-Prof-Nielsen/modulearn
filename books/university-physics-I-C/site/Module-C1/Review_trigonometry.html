C2.1 Review of Required Trigonometry#

2.1.1 Motivation#

As we start using vectors (directed line segments) to represent physical quantities, for example velocity, we will need to have sufficient skills to break them into components (1D objects) that are suitable for additiona and subtraction. For example, we will need to break force vectors into \(x\) and \(y\)-components to be able to quantify Newton’s 2nd law.

In this course, we will need to two skill set from geometry and trigonometry:

  • Pythagorean theorem

  • Trigonometric functions applied to a right-angle triangle

C2.2.2 Pythagorean Theorem#

The Pythagorean theorem allows us to relate the three lengths of a right-angle triangle through the following relation:

Pythagorean Theorem
\[ a^2 + b^2 = c^2 \]

where \(a\) and \(b\) sides adjacent to the right angle and \(c\) is the opposite side to the right angle.

C2.2.3 Trigonometric Functions#

We will be using three trigonometric functions: \(\cos()\), \(\sin()\), and \(\tan()\).

These three functions can be defined in terms of the sides and an angle in a right-angle triangle. The sides are often referred to as the adjacent, opposite, and hypotenuse in relation to the angle \(\alpha\).

Definitions
\[ \cos\alpha = \frac{Adj}{Hyp} \]
\[ \sin\alpha = \frac{Opp}{Hyp} \]
\[ \tan\alpha = \frac{Opp}{Adj} \]

In addition, we have the following relationship that is helpful at times:

\[ \tan\alpha = \frac{\sin\alpha}{\cos\alpha} \]

C2.2.4 Units of Angle#

While the sides of the triangle will have the units of the physical parameter represented, the angle is expressed in two different units: degrees (\(^\circ\)) or radians (rad). These units can be related by considering a circle. The full angle around in a circle spans \(360^\circ\) or \(2\pi\) rad. The relationships therefore is

\[ 360^\circ = 2\pi~\textrm{rad} \]

We will be using both degrees and radians on this course.
