Dissemination Outcomes#

Purpose#

The dissemination component of this project is intended to:

  • share lessons learned from implementing AI-supported project-based learning,

  • explore responsible AI integration in STEM education,

  • discuss AI-aware assessment practices,

  • and contribute to ongoing discussions about computational and AI-supported scientific workflows in physics education.

The project is designed not only as a classroom implementation effort, but also as a broader pedagogical exploration of how AI can be integrated into higher education while preserving:

  • scientific reasoning,

  • intellectual ownership,

  • and authentic scientific practice.

Potential Dissemination Formats#

Dissemination outcomes may include:

  • conference presentations,

  • poster presentations,

  • faculty workshops,

  • invited discussions,

  • departmental seminars,

  • teaching demonstrations,

  • or future peer-reviewed publications.

The framework is especially well suited for dissemination within communities focused on:

  • physics education,

  • computational physics,

  • STEM pedagogy,

  • AI in education,

  • project-based learning,

  • and competency-based learning.

Possible Conference Themes#

Potential dissemination topics include:

  • AI-supported project-based learning in introductory physics

  • Computational physics as a Phase A synthesis assessment

  • Responsible AI use in STEM education

  • AI-aware assessment design

  • Preserving intellectual ownership in AI-supported classrooms

  • AI-supported computational workflows using Jupyter notebooks

  • Monte Carlo uncertainty analysis in introductory computational physics

  • Orbital dynamics as a computational research project

  • Scientific synthesis in introductory physics courses

  • Balancing AI scaffolding versus intellectual outsourcing

Conference Presentation Possibilities#

Oral Presentations#

An oral presentation could discuss:

  • development of the AI mentor framework,

  • implementation challenges,

  • AI-aware assessment design,

  • lessons learned from pilot testing,

  • and computational project structure.

Poster Presentations#

Poster presentations may focus on:

  • the orbital dynamics computational module,

  • AI-supported scientific workflows,

  • Jupyter notebook integration,

  • uncertainty analysis,

  • project structure,

  • and assessment philosophy.

Faculty Workshops#

The project framework may also be adapted into faculty workshops discussing:

  • responsible AI use,

  • AI-supported project-based learning,

  • computational physics implementation,

  • AI-aware assessment strategies,

  • and scaffolded research workflows.

Student Communication Formats#

The final student project may be communicated through:

  • written scientific reports,

  • scientific posters,

  • oral presentations using slides,

  • or oral examinations with faculty.

The oral examination option is particularly useful when:

  • a traditional presentation format is not appropriate,

  • project scope is highly individualized,

  • or deeper questioning is desired to evaluate scientific understanding.

Oral examinations may include:

  • discussion of methodology,

  • explanation of numerical methods,

  • interpretation of uncertainty analysis,

  • explanation of computational choices,

  • and reflection on AI-supported workflow decisions.

This format can provide stronger evidence of:

  • conceptual understanding,

  • intellectual ownership,

  • and authentic scientific reasoning.

Peer-Reviewed Publication Possibilities#

Future peer-reviewed dissemination could explore:

  • AI-supported computational physics pedagogy,

  • AI-aware assessment frameworks,

  • responsible AI use in STEM education,

  • computational project-based learning,

  • or scientific synthesis in introductory physics.

Possible article themes may include:

  • preserving intellectual ownership in AI-supported environments,

  • constrained AI mentor architectures,

  • computational modeling in introductory physics,

  • or uncertainty analysis through project-based learning.

Educational Significance#

One major outcome of this project is the recognition that AI integration in STEM education requires more than simply allowing AI use.

The project highlights the need for:

  • carefully constrained AI workflows,

  • AI-aware assessment,

  • process-focused evaluation,

  • and preservation of student scientific reasoning.

Final Perspective#

The dissemination component of this project is not simply about presenting a new classroom activity.

It is about contributing to a larger educational question:

How can AI-supported computational workflows be integrated into STEM education while still preserving scientific reasoning, intellectual ownership, and authentic learning?

The framework developed through this project attempts to address that question by combining:

  • computational physics,

  • project-based learning,

  • responsible AI use,

  • AI-aware assessment,

  • and scientific synthesis

within a modern scientific learning environment.
