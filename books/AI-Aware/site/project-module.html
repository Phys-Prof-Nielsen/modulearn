PHASE A RESEARCH PROJECT - Computational Physics Research Project#

Project Overview#

In this Phase A project, you will design and investigate your own computational physics research project.

This project is designed to simulate authentic scientific and engineering workflows. Rather than solving a standard textbook problem, you will:

  • develop a research question,

  • construct computational models,

  • analyze numerical methods,

  • investigate uncertainty,

  • interpret evidence critically,

  • and communicate scientific conclusions professionally.

Your project should integrate:

  • physics theory,

  • computational modeling,

  • numerical methods,

  • uncertainty analysis,

  • and scientific reasoning

into a coherent scientific investigation.

The project emphasizes:

  • scientific synthesis,

  • computational reasoning,

  • scientific methodology,

  • and responsible use of AI.

AI-Supported Scientific Workflow (“Vibe Coding”)#

This project intentionally incorporates responsible AI-supported computational work, sometimes informally referred to as “vibe coding.”

In professional scientific and engineering environments, researchers increasingly use AI tools to:

  • brainstorm ideas,

  • debug code,

  • explain concepts,

  • organize workflows,

  • suggest computational approaches,

  • and accelerate development.

However, AI should support scientific thinking — not replace it.

For this project:

  • YOU must remain the scientific decision maker.

  • YOU must understand the physics, methodology, assumptions, and computational work.

  • YOU must verify correctness and interpret results.

AI is a tool, not a substitute for scientific reasoning.

Required AI Research Mentor#

You are required to use the custom ChatGPT “research mentor” (architect) developed specifically for this course.

The architect is designed to:

  • guide your thinking,

  • ask questions,

  • critique vague ideas,

  • identify weaknesses,

  • help refine methodology,

  • and support scientific reasoning.

The architect is NOT designed to:

  • give you answers,

  • choose your project,

  • write your proposal,

  • write your code,

  • or make scientific decisions for you.

The goal is for you to develop the intellectual ownership required for authentic scientific work.

You are expected to actively collaborate with the architect rather than passively asking it to complete tasks.

Link to the custom architect

Important AI Rules#

The architect will intentionally refuse requests such as:

  • “write this section for me,”

  • “choose the best method for me,”

  • “give me the answer,”

  • “pick the variables,”

  • “design the project,”

  • or similar requests.

This is intentional.

The purpose of the project is for YOU to demonstrate:

  • scientific reasoning,

  • methodological decision making,

  • computational thinking,

  • and scientific synthesis.

The architect may help you:

  • refine ideas,

  • critique decisions,

  • identify missing components,

  • improve organization,

  • and strengthen your reasoning.

But the intellectual work must remain student-driven.

Learning Objectives#

By completing this project, you should be able to:

  1. Develop a computational physics research question.

  2. Apply physics principles to a real or simulated physical system.

  3. Implement numerical methods in Python.

  4. Construct and evaluate computational models.

  5. Analyze uncertainty using statistical and Monte Carlo methods.

  6. Interpret computational and/or experimental results using evidence and physics reasoning.

  7. Evaluate assumptions, limitations, and numerical behavior.

  8. Communicate scientific methodology and conclusions clearly.

  9. Reflect critically on responsible AI-supported scientific workflows.

Choosing a Project Topic#

You are expected to choose your own project topic.

Projects may involve:

  • mechanics,

  • orbital dynamics,

  • oscillations,

  • fluids,

  • thermodynamics,

  • waves,

  • numerical simulations,

  • biomechanics,

  • atmospheric physics,

  • sports physics,

  • or other approved physics-related systems.

Your project may involve:

  • experimental data,

  • publicly available datasets,

  • simulations,

  • or combinations of data and modeling.

Example Project Topic#

One example project is computational orbital dynamics.

For example, students might investigate:

  • numerical stability of orbital simulations,

  • conservation of energy and angular momentum,

  • timestep sensitivity,

  • uncertainty propagation,

  • or comparison of numerical integration methods.

For a two-body gravitational system:

\[ F = \frac{GMm}{r^2} \]

Newton’s second law gives:

\[ m\vec{a} = \vec{F} \]

which leads to:

\[ \vec{a} = -\frac{GM}{r^3}\vec{r} \]

This is only ONE example project direction.

You are encouraged to develop your own scientific investigation.

Project Structure#

The project is divided into several stages.

At each stage, you are expected to make the scientific decisions.

The AI mentor will guide your thinking, but it will not complete the project for you.

Stage 1 — Research Question Development#

You will develop a research question related to your chosen physical system and computational investigation.

Possible broad themes include:

  • numerical stability,

  • conservation laws,

  • uncertainty propagation,

  • model limitations,

  • parameter sensitivity,

  • comparison of numerical methods,

  • or model-to-data comparison.

You should think about:

  • What specifically are you investigating?

  • What quantities will you compare?

  • What evidence will answer your question?

  • What makes the question testable?

You should expect the AI mentor to ask you many questions during this stage.

Stage 2 — Computational Model Development#

You will develop a computational model using Python.

Possible numerical methods may include:

  • Euler,

  • Euler-Cromer,

  • Verlet,

  • RK4,

  • finite-difference methods,

  • Monte Carlo methods,

  • or other methods you choose and justify.

You must justify:

  • your numerical method,

  • assumptions,

  • parameter choices,

  • timestep choices,

  • and computational approach.

The AI mentor may help you evaluate your reasoning, but you must make the decisions.

Stage 3 — Data Analysis#

You will decide:

  • what quantities to track,

  • what graphs to create,

  • what comparisons to make,

  • and how to evaluate your model.

Depending on your project, possible quantities might include:

  • energy,

  • momentum,

  • position,

  • velocity,

  • angular momentum,

  • temperature,

  • oscillation amplitude,

  • probability distributions,

  • or numerical error.

You should focus on scientific interpretation, not simply generating plots.

Stage 4 — Monte Carlo Uncertainty Analysis#

You are required to include advanced uncertainty analysis using Monte Carlo methods.

You should investigate:

  • sensitivity to parameters,

  • propagation of uncertainty,

  • robustness of conclusions,

  • and limitations of the model.

For example:

\[ x \rightarrow x + \delta x \]
\[ v \rightarrow v + \delta v \]

The goal is not simply to “run simulations,” but to interpret uncertainty scientifically.

Stage 5 — Scientific Synthesis#

Your project should integrate:

  • physics theory,

  • computational modeling,

  • uncertainty analysis,

  • numerical methods,

  • and scientific interpretation

into one coherent scientific investigation.

This project is NOT simply a coding assignment.

The emphasis is on:

  • scientific reasoning,

  • methodological justification,

  • interpretation of evidence,

  • and synthesis of multiple approaches.

Final Communication Product#

You must submit ONE professional scientific communication product:

  • written scientific report,

  • scientific poster,

  • oral presentation with slides,

  • or oral examination/interview with faculty.

The oral examination option may be used when:

  • a traditional presentation format is not appropriate,

  • the project is highly individualized,

  • or deeper questioning is desired to evaluate understanding.

Your final product must include:

  • motivation,

  • research question,

  • theoretical background,

  • methodology,

  • computational approach,

  • uncertainty analysis,

  • graphs and visualizations,

  • interpretation of results,

  • limitations,

  • and responsible AI reflection.

Code should be included in an appendix or linked repository.

Datasets should be embedded or linked appropriately.

Responsible AI Reflection#

You are required to include a reflection discussing how AI was used.

Your reflection should discuss:

  • what AI assistance you used,

  • what decisions remained student-made,

  • how you verified correctness,

  • how AI helped your workflow,

  • and how you maintained intellectual ownership of the project.

You should think critically about both:

  • the strengths of AI support,

  • and the limitations or risks of overreliance.

Suggested Computational Tools#

Recommended Python libraries include:

  • numpy

  • matplotlib

  • scipy

  • pandas

Optional:

  • sympy

  • plotly

  • numba

You should clearly document:

  • assumptions,

  • parameter choices,

  • numerical methods,

  • and computational workflow.

Assessment Emphasis#

Assessment will emphasize:

  • scientific reasoning,

  • methodological justification,

  • computational implementation,

  • uncertainty analysis,

  • interpretation of evidence,

  • and scientific communication.

Assessment will NOT emphasize:

  • obtaining a single “correct” answer,

  • polished AI-generated writing,

  • or project complexity for its own sake.

Final Remarks#

This project is intended to model authentic scientific practice.

Scientists and engineers do not simply solve predefined textbook problems. They:

  • ask questions,

  • make methodological decisions,

  • evaluate uncertainty,

  • critique assumptions,

  • interpret evidence,

  • and communicate conclusions.

This project is designed to help you practice those skills while responsibly integrating modern AI-supported computational workflows.
