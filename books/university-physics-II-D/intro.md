# A Competency Approach to University Physics II: A Compendium to the D Exam

Phase D of University Physics II is designed to establish the foundational tools and perspectives 
needed to study electric and magnetic fields, waves, and the first ideas of modern physics. 
Unlike Phase D in my University Physics I course, physical situations in this phase are not restricted to one dimension. 
You will work naturally with vectors and introductory calculus, using 
them as practical tools to describe how physical quantities vary in space and time.

Because students enter Physics II with diverse backgrounds, Phase D revisits core 
habits of quantitative reasoning while extending them into new contexts. Key ideas 
in electromagnetism, wave phenomena, and energy transport are introduced in their 
simplest and most transparent forms, emphasizing physical meaning over algebraic 
complexity. Mathematical expressions are treated as models of physical relationships, 
not just formulas to memorize.

Alongside the physics, you will continue to develop computational fluency using 
Python and Jupyter notebooks. Computation is used as a natural extension of analytical 
reasoning—to visualize fields, explore parameter dependence, check limiting cases, and 
connect equations to physical behavior. You are assumed to be familiar with basic 
statistical ideas from Physics I and laboratory work; here, computation is focused on 
modeling and interpretation rather than data fundamentals.

The goal of Phase D is to build confidence and coherence: confidence in using 
vectors, calculus, and computation together, and coherence in understanding how the 
core ideas of Physics II fit into a unified framework. This phase lays the groundwork 
for deeper theoretical structure and more sophisticated problem solving in the 
later phases of the course.
